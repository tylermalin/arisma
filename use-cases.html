Where machine settlement pays off first.

Seven industries where machines and agents already do the work, and still wait on invoices, cards, and people to get paid.

platform / use cases

The pattern behind every strong fit.

Arisma earns its place where four things are true at once. The more of them a workflow has, the more cost and delay it removes.

  1. 01

    It happens constantly

    Thousands of events a day per fleet, too many to invoice one by one.

  2. 02

    Each event is worth little

    Fractions of a cent to a few dollars, below what card fees allow.

  3. 03

    No person is present

    The payer is a machine or an agent acting under someone else's authority.

  4. 04

    Something physical must be proven

    Payment should follow a verified event, not a claim that it happened.

01

AI agents & compute

Agents that pay for their own inference, data, and work.

An agent calls models, buys datasets, and hires other agents to finish a task. Today it borrows a shared API key billed to a corporate card, so no one can say what a single agent spent or stop it at a limit. With Arisma each agent gets its own budget bound session key, pays per request, and holds funds in escrow until a hired agent delivers.

Who pays whom
Agent to model, data, or agent provider
What settles
Per request, or escrow released on delivery
What it replaces
Shared keys, card billing, monthly reconciliation
  1. delegate Owner grants a key: settle only, $25 cap, one hour.
  2. settle Each model call clears as it happens.
  3. verify Escrow for hired work releases when output checks pass.
Agent Escrow MCP

per call metered

Highest volume of the seven: software agents transact at machine speed with no person in the loop.

On the X1PCIe or shared memory ingest. The TPM signs each inference output with a hash of its input.

02

Logistics & warehousing

Robots billed per pick, settled per pick.

Robots as a service contracts charge per pick, per tote, or per hour, then reconcile a monthly invoice against the operator's own logs. Disputes come from two sets of numbers. With Arisma the robot fleet signs each completed pick in its enclave and the warehouse settles it in the same second, so both sides hold the same receipt.

Who pays whom
Warehouse operator to robot fleet owner
What settles
Each pick, streamed
What it replaces
Monthly invoices and log reconciliation
  1. attest The arm's enclave signs the completed pick.
  2. settle $0.002 clears to the fleet owner.
  3. anchor Both parties keep one shared receipt.
IX-02 robot handoff

$0.002 per pick

Very high frequency with tiny values, the exact shape card rails and invoices handle worst.

On the X1ROS2 node over DDS or CAN FD. Navigation strings carry a sequence counter, so replays fail.

03

EV charging & fleet energy

Autonomous fleets that charge without an app or a card.

A robotaxi has no driver to tap a card or open an app, and fleets juggle charging networks, roaming fees, and settlements that land days later. With Arisma the vehicle presents a scoped key at the charger and pays per metered kWh while it charges. The same path pays a home battery or depot for a verified kWh delivered back to the grid during a demand response event.

Who pays whom
Vehicle to charger; grid operator to battery
What settles
Metered kWh, during the session
What it replaces
Charging cards, roaming hubs, delayed payouts
  1. delegate The fleet issues the vehicle a charging only key.
  2. settle $0.31 clears for every kWh the meter signs.
  3. anchor The session receipt closes when the plug comes out.
IX-03 vehicle energy

$0.31 per kWh

Larger tickets, no person at the pump, and a meter that can sign its own readings.

On the X1OCPP 2.0.1 and ISO 15118. Billing and state of charge tokens are signed before they leave the site.

04

Drone delivery & inspection

Pay for the inspection once the imagery proves it happened.

Utilities and insurers pay drone operators to inspect lines, towers, and roofs, then wait for a report before they trust the job was done. With Arisma the drone anchors each capture with a C2PA provenance hash, the gate checks that the frames match the flight, and payment releases on the verified capture. Between jobs the same drone pays for its own charge at the pad.

Who pays whom
Utility or insurer to drone operator; drone to pad
What settles
Each verified capture, each charge
What it replaces
Manual report review before invoicing
  1. attest Each frame carries a C2PA hash at capture.
  2. verify The gate matches frames to the flight plan.
  3. settle Payment releases; the pad charge clears at $0.14.
IX-01 drone charge

1.4s finality

The value sits in proof of work done in the field, which C2PA anchoring makes checkable.

On the X1MAVLink and RTSP. Video is encrypted with ephemeral keys and GPS is signed at the dock.

05

Cold chain & pharmaceuticals

Release payment only if the shipment stayed in range.

Vaccines and biologics must stay within a set temperature band, and a single excursion can void a shipment. Today that is proven after the fact from data loggers and paperwork. With Arisma the carrier proves in zero knowledge that every reading stayed between 2 and 8 °C without handing over the full trace, and a quality lead holds the final release, as pharmaceutical batch release already requires.

Who pays whom
Shipper to carrier, on proven compliance
What settles
The shipment, at delivery
What it replaces
Logger downloads, paperwork, claims disputes
  1. attest Sensors anchor readings with C2PA in transit.
  2. verify A zero knowledge proof shows the band held.
  3. gate A person approves release, then payment settles.
Human in the loop gate

2 to 8 °C proven privately

High value per shipment and a regulated human sign off, which the gate is built to hold.

On the X1BLE 5.2 or 1-Wire sensors. Each reading is chained to a secure timestamp, so edits break the chain.

06

Environmental monitoring & MRV

Sensors that sell verified readings, one at a time.

Carbon, water, and air quality programs depend on measurement, reporting, and verification, and buyers increasingly ask where each number came from. With Arisma a field sensor signs every reading at the source, anchors it with C2PA, and sells it per reading to whoever needs it: a registry, a buyer, an insurer, or a research team.

Who pays whom
Data buyer to sensor owner
What settles
Each verified reading
What it replaces
Bulk data licenses and manual audits
  1. attest The sensor signs the reading in hardware.
  2. anchor A C2PA hash ties it to time and place.
  3. settle $0.0004 clears to the sensor owner.
IX-04 sensor telemetry

$0.0004 per reading

The data is the product, so provenance at the source is worth more than the payment itself.

On the X1LoRaWAN or Modbus RTU. Readings are batched into one signed digest per interval.

07

Industrial machinery & manufacturing

Machines sold by the hour, cycle, or part, with a person on the switch.

Compressors, machine tools, and heavy equipment are moving to pay per use, billed from usage reports the customer has to trust. With Arisma the machine signs each cycle and settles it as it runs. Before a heavy machine starts, the gate waits for a named operator to approve, so automation never removes the person responsible for safety.

Who pays whom
Plant to equipment owner
What settles
Each cycle, hour, or finished part
What it replaces
Usage reports and quarterly true ups
  1. gate An operator approves activation.
  2. attest The controller signs each completed cycle.
  3. settle Usage clears as the machine runs.
Policy gate

per cycle metered

Equipment as a service needs trusted usage data and a hard safety stop. Both are built in.

On the X1OPC UA, Modbus TCP, or EtherCAT. Legacy PLC links end inside the gateway and leave over mTLS.
