The Arisma mandate stack.

Three layers: who a machine acts for, how it pays, and what evidence it leaves.

platform / architecture

Machines need three things card rails were never built for: authority they can prove with no person present, payments small enough to price a single action, and a worst case set before they start.

Three layers.

Mandates

A mandate is a scoped, expiring permission on a smart account. The account uses ERC-7702 to point at Arisma's permission module. The mandate sets a spend cap, a rate, an expiry, and allowed counterparties. The machine signs with a P-256 key held in hardware, and the chain checks that signature through the RIP-7212 precompile.

Settlement

Payments settle in USDC on Base. A single payment confirms in about 1.4 seconds and becomes final when its batch finalizes on Ethereum. High-frequency payments, per pick or per reading, are netted under the mandate's limits and settled in batches. Receipts are deterministic, so anyone holding the inputs can recompute them.

Evidence and release

Each payment can carry evidence: a signed reading from an Arisma gateway, a C2PA manifest on a drone capture, or a policy proof. High-stakes physical actions, releasing a payload or starting heavy machinery, wait for a person to approve. Payments do not.

SEV-SNP and TDX are data center technologies. Devices that cannot run them sit behind an EnclaveEdge X1, whose TPM holds the key.

Under ERC-7702 the account's original key keeps full authority and can change delegation at any time. Keep it in a hardware wallet. A leaked mandate is bounded; a leaked owner key is not.
